# Day 22 Commands — Controlling Processes with Signals

1. `kill` — It sends a signal to a process using its PID.
2. `kill -9` — This forcefully terminates a process.
3. `kill -HUP` — It sends a hangup signal, commonly used to make a process reload its configuration.
4. `killall` — It sends a signal to processes by name.
5. `pkill` — This finds processes by name or other criteria and sends a signal.
6. `fg` — It brings a background job to the foreground.
7. `bg` — It resumes a suspended job in the background.
8. `Ctrl+Z` — This suspends the currently running foreground process.
9. `nohup` — This keeps a command running even after logging out.
10. `disown` — It removes a job from the shell's job list so it is not affected when the shell exits.
